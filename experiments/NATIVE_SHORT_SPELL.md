# Native-rate H/A emission refinement

This experiment tested whether instantaneous tracking geometry at each provider's native sampling rate can restore quick H/A turnovers that a frozen 5 Hz GRU prior may smooth away. The candidate adds a small, provider-held-out logistic residual to the frozen prior's H/A log odds. It uses 23 instantaneous control-geometry fields and their finite masks, with no contact history, temporal-window shift, boundary model, or GRU refit. The archived D probability is copied exactly; only H/A mass is redistributed. Evaluation used the existing cost-3 decoder without changes.

The resolution comparison was frozen before fitting: `native_rate` trains on every native row; `matched_5hz` uses the same head, feature set, and optimizer budget on rows sampled at 5 Hz, then interpolates only the residual within continuous time blocks before adding it to the native prior. Provider rates are Sportec 25 Hz, Metrica 25 Hz, and SkillCorner 10 Hz, so “native” does not mean 25 Hz for every provider.

Both arms used two backbone seeds, three provider-held-out folds, equal provider → match → augmentation-view → team-swap support, 512 optimizer updates per head, and batches of 2,048. The twelve fitted residual heads were sealed before the single declared evaluation pass. The native heads saw about 2.92–3.38 million augmented rows per fold/seed; the 5 Hz heads saw about 0.63–1.15 million. Row counts differ by temporal resolution, while every active provider has equal total sample weight and the held-out provider has zero weight.

Each prior was generated by its matching provider-held-out frozen GRU checkpoint over TRAIN caches only; no evaluation archive contributed to prior or residual fitting.

## Results

The saved ensemble baseline remains the strongest primary result. The native-rate residual did not meet the predeclared clean spell-F1 requirement and scored slightly below the matched 5 Hz residual on both aggregate views.

Short-spell recall is diagnostic, not the selection metric. Native-rate clean recall fell by 0.006030 against baseline and by 0.001929 against the matched 5 Hz arm. Feed-like recall rose by 0.004307 against baseline and 0.001192 against matched 5 Hz, without improving feed-like spell F1.

The native clean macro-F1 and D-F1 guards passed for every provider: the worst provider deltas were −0.001520 macro-F1 and −0.001308 D-F1, both within the −0.02 floor. The feed-like spell-F1 guard (no worse than −0.01) also passed. The clean spell-F1 criterion (at least +0.01 over baseline) failed, so the experiment does not support replacing or promoting the saved ensemble. The matched 5 Hz arm failed that clean criterion as well.

Native-rate clean spell-F1 deltas against the matched arm varied by provider: Metrica −0.006103, SkillCorner +0.001763, and Sportec +0.003390. This is not consistent evidence that the native timing itself recovers quick turnovers. The aggregate result is slightly worse, and native-rate short-spell recall is also lower on clean data.

## Validation and provenance

The scoring pass covered 15 cells per arm: five clean and ten feed-like. It produced 30 ensemble rows and 60 single-seed diagnostic rows. All 30 archived ensemble D-probability checks passed exactly. The readout records the per-provider and per-seed scores, baseline rows, decoded predictions, and probability artifacts. No organizer labels or scores were used, and no automatic promotion occurred.

The frozen declaration and source/train inputs verified before fitting and after scoring. A pre-fit validation assertion originally expected mass from all three providers even though each leave-one-provider-out training fold contains only two. The original source and declaration remain unchanged. The reviewed runtime amendment's only in-memory code correction changes the checks to require mass 1 for each active provider, zero for the held-out provider, and total active mass 2. It also binds the separate prior-scheduling wrapper, original/effective function hashes, all six fold-by-arm mass checks, and the prior cache/receipt hashes. Three prior caches were reused from the earlier single-worker preparation and three were computed by the bounded three-process scheduler. No feature values, targets, weights, loss, or optimizer settings changed. The amendment review and launch checks passed before fitting.
